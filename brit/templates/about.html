History

BRIT-EU

2024–2026

From December 2024 to May 2026, Hamburg University of Technology (TUHH) and the European Compost Network (ECN) developed BRIT further in BRIT-EU. The project expanded the household waste collection data model and introduced a contributor submission and review workflow and the digital Waste Atlas.

Structured data, source references and exports supported comparisons of biowaste collection systems and case studies in Catalonia, North Rhine-Westphalia, Rhineland-Palatinate, Sweden and South Tyrol. Development and data curation have continued beyond the funded project period.

BRIT-EU project website

CLOSECYCLE

2024–2027

CLOSECYCLE (2024 to 2027) is an Interreg North Sea project, co-funded by the European Union, working towards Territorial Biorefinery Networks (TBNs) that turn local residual bioresources into useful products. BRIT forms part of its digital tool package. The work includes a revised Materials model for samples, analytical measurements, units, reference bases and sources; a Processes module linking input and output materials with operating conditions; and mapped showcases of territorial biorefinery networks. Partner data and process descriptions are being curated and reviewed.

CLOSECYCLE project website

SOILCOM

BRIT was significantly improved and extended in the SOILCOM project. The functionality of the core modules was adapted to fit the special needs of modelling the SOILCOM waste stream model. Further, the tool was extended with a completely new Household Waste Collection module and an interface with SimuCF, a simulation tool for aerobic and anaerobic digestion processes. To find out more about the SOILCOM project and the associated partners, please visit the project website below.

SOILCOM project website

SOILCOM is an Interreg North Sea Region project.

FLEXIBI

The BRIT prototype was developed as part of the Decision Support Tool of the FLEXIBI project. The development in FLEXIBI included the core modules "Bibliography", "Maps", "Materials" and "Inventories" as well as models and data for the case studies "Greenhouses in Nantes" and "Roadside trees in Hamburg".
